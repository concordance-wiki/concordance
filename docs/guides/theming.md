# Theming

The generated site is a set of named slots rendered at build by Preact components. A project restyles it with `theme.yaml` and a stylesheet; a plugin replaces any slot with its own component. This page states the contract: the slots, their view models, how an override is declared, how the stylesheet is layered and what the budget measures. The view models are part of plugin API version `1`; a change of shape bumps it.

## Slots

| Slot | Renders | Composes |
|---|---|---|
| `Shell` | the document: `<html lang dir>`, the head assets (favicon, the mode script, stylesheets, bundles), the skip link, the body | everything |
| `Header` | the top bar: the mark and the site title linking home, the search field with its `/` shortcut (confined to the space on a space page), the navigation (the spaces page, the A–Z index, the recent changes), the mode switch; no statistic; on a narrow screen a menu button opening the drawer that holds the search field, the spaces with their pages, the tree of the current space and the links | — |
| `Footer` | two columns then a line: what the tool knows (the build instant, the repositories counted and linked to the spaces page, the link to the about page, the generator and its licence), what the organisation declared (the legal pages of the configuration, the project text and links), shown only when something was; the build line with the profile, the page count and the to-do page with its count | — |
| `Home` | the question and its explanation, the search field drawn large with its live results in the flow of the page, the most cited pages as chips, the spaces, each row leading to the page of its space, the recent changes and the alerts on dormant spaces | — |
| `EntityPage` | the tree of the space on the left; the breadcrumb, the title, the line naming the type, the last change and the space, the note as an article with its legend, the documents of the entity (download links, the viewer opened on demand, the rail of positions, the extracted text), the path of the file with its edit link; on the right the properties, the table of contents, the related pages and the neighbourhood folded behind its line, the map taking the panel once unfolded; an `api` whose contract was imported gets the API page instead (`ApiPage`, served by the same slot): its operations under it in the tree, no highlight, the operations table and the contract block after the note, the properties cut to five keys and the operations first among the related pages | `Neighbourhood`, `MentionsPanel` |
| `KeywordPage` | the tree of the space the word is filed in on the left; the breadcrumb, the dotted title, the "no definition" mark and since when the word is used, the notice with the lead to propose a definition, the passages by page with their type, their title, their count and where each one stands, two in view and the others folded, the files beyond six behind a disclosure; on the right what we know, the expressions that may be the same thing, the related pages, the neighbourhood folded, drawn from the words that accompany the word | `Neighbourhood`, `MentionsPanel` |
| `MentionsPanel` | the related pages: one entry per page that evokes the entity, most passages first, with its type, its count and an excerpt, "cited" when the page writes a link; the first `initial` mentions inline, the rest from the fragment of the entity; a text filter and a type filter once the island runs | — |
| `Neighbourhood` | the map of the neighbourhood with its controls and its legend, and its textual equivalent, the list of neighbours | — |
| `SearchResults` | the facets in the left column, the active filters as chips with the summary, the ordered results as cards, the closest form when nothing matched | — |
| `Index` | the title and the sentence counting the words and the notes, the filters folded behind their button, the letters, active or inactive, with the count of the letters without an entry, and the entries of one segment as a table of words, types, first lines and citing pages under a heading per letter: the whole index, anchored letter by letter, or one letter of a segmented index | — |
| `Todo` | documents without markdown, words without a note, suspected noise folded | — |
| `Spaces` | the spaces page: every space in one table, the most cited first, with its badge and name, its content, its page count and its newest change, a dormant space dated in the accent and in days; the note on the dates | — |
| `Space` | the page of one space: the breadcrumb from the spaces page, the badge, the title, the declared description, the line counting the pages, naming the repository and dating the newest change; the categories of the repository, each opening its list; the latest changes with their category, the most cited words counted in the space only, the closing sentence; no tree | — |
| `CategoryList` | the list of a folder of a space, at any depth, or of a year or a month of a dated space: the tree of the space on the left, the folders on the way open, this folder marked; the breadcrumb, the title, the line counting the notes and describing the type, the selectors of the highlighted attribute and of the sort, the table of notes with the attribute, the first line and the number of related pages of each, the rows shown of the whole, the page links and the note on the links column; the selectors link to pre-rendered variants, or apply their choice in place through the `category-list` island when the variants would be too many | — |

`renderPage(slot, props, options)` renders any slot but `Shell` as a complete page: the shell wraps the header, a `<main id="main">` holding the page, and the footer. `renderSlot(slot, props, theme)` renders one slot alone, for tests and galleries. A page component that needs a panel asks the theme for it with `useSlot("MentionsPanel")`, so that an override of the panel applies inside every page.

An `Attribute` is `{ name, label, values: { text, href? }[] }`, `label` being what the profile declares for the attribute in the site language, else its name; the default component separates the values of a list with a comma. A `SpaceNode` is `{ label, href?, count?, children?, current?, omitted? }`: a folder has a `count` of pages and, when it lies on the way to the current page, its `children`; a page has an `href`, or `current` when it is the page shown; an `omitted` node stands for the pages of a long folder the tree leaves out, its label saying how many (the tree lists at most `SPACE_PAGES_MAX`, forty, around the current page). A `TypeDeclaration` is `{ type, label, group, glyph?, attributes: { name, label, type, target?, relation?, values? }[], sections: { key, heading, parse, produces }[], display: { highlight, neighboursOrder } }`, everything a dedicated component needs to lay a page out from the declaration rather than from a hard-coded list of keys. A `HomeSpace` is `{ name, href, initials, count, unit: "pages" | "documents", countLabel?, date?, dateLabel?, stale }`: `href` leads to the page of the space, `unit` says what the count counts, documents when the notes of the space mostly stand for converted documents, `countLabel` is the count worded in the site language, `date` and `dateLabel` the newest change of the space and that change worded relative to the build ("2 days ago"), and `stale` whether the staleness threshold makes it dormant. A `SpaceRow` is `{ name, href, initials, content, count, date?, dateLabel?, stale }`, `content` being the sentence the configuration declares for the source or the labels of its dominant types, and `dateLabel` reading in days ("193 days ago") when `stale` is true, so that the accent of the date is doubled by a number. The `dateLabel` strings are worded in the project locale; a component shows the ISO value when they are absent. The `html` of a section is the markdown already rendered by the build; a theme inserts it as is. Every list arrives in its final order; a component never sorts.

### The entity page

One template serves every type. Its order is imposed, and the default component keeps it in the markup so that a reader, a screen reader and the search index meet the same page: inside `<main>`, a `<div class="entity">` (`.entity-with-space` when the page has a space) holding three regions. First the left column, a `<nav class="space">` named "Tree of the space": a `<details class="space-tree">` whose summary is the initials badge and the name of the space, then the `<ul class="space-nodes">` of its folders with their counts, the folders on the way to the page open, the current page a `<span aria-current="page">` ruled and bold rather than coloured. Then the centre, `<div class="entity-main">`: the `<nav class="breadcrumbs">` (space › folders › page), a `<header>` with the `<h1>` and, under it, the line naming the type badge (`<a class="badge">` to the results filtered on the type when the page knows where), the last change as a `<time class="entity-changed">`, the space as `<a class="entity-space">` reading "Space <title>" and leading to its page, and nothing else (`.entity-badge`): the properties the profile puts forward stay in the panel, which says them once; right after it, the note as an `<article class="entity-body">` at full column width, one `<section id="section-…">` per heading with the rendered HTML in a `.markdown` block; then the `<footer class="entity-footer">` with the `<p class="legend">` naming the two marks of the text ("written link", "recognised word", `entity.legendWritten` and `entity.legendRecognised`), the path of the file in the monospace family at the end of the line, linked to the file on its forge when the forge is known, and, on the note, "Something to correct? Edit this page" when the call to action has somewhere to lead: the forge, else `project.contribute_url`; the link carries the short label "Edit" too, which the phone shows in place of the long one next to the name of the file. Then the right column, `<div class="entity-side">`: the blocks of the panel, each a `<section class="panel-block">` (or the `<aside class="mentions">`) holding a `<details class="panel-fold">` whose summary is the `<h2>` of the block, in this order: the declared metadata as a description list under "Properties" with the note "N declared keys. The rest of the file is free text." (`entity.declaredAtTop`, worded with the count of the list), the attributes the type does not declare under "Other attributes" when the note sets some, the table of contents under "On this page" (one link per section with a heading), the related pages, and last the `<details class="neighbourhood-fold">` whose summary reads "See the neighbourhood map · N pages" and which holds the neighbourhood slot; `mapOpen` serves that fold open, as the gallery does for its `entity-page-map` state. The DOM order never puts metadata between the title and the text; the [layout](#the-default-theme) below says how the three regions sit at every width.

What changes from one type to another comes from the profile only: the label of the badge, the labels of the attributes and the order of the neighbours (`display.neighbours_order`, applied by the model). `display.highlight` changes nothing on the generic page, whose line under the title names the type, the last change and the space alone: the profile key leads the five keys of the [API page](#the-contract-viewer) and gives the [list of a category](#the-category-list) its filter and its second column. A written reference among the values of the panel is resolved as the pipeline resolves it, by its identifier as written, else by the identifier it takes within the source of the note (`roles/publication/reader` from a note of `specs`), and shown as the title of its note, linked; a value naming no note stays as written. The properties block (`.entity-panel`) holds the common properties then the attributes the type declares, in declaration order; the frontmatter keys the profile declares for no type follow in a second block (`Other attributes`, `.entity-others`), as written, so that a note of any type shows everything its author wrote even when nothing declares it, and so that a project sees what it could still declare. A test renders two entities of different types and checks that the markup differs nowhere else; another checks that no type slug appears in the default theme. The generic page is the fallback of every type; the section below says how a type gets a page of its own.

### Rendering per type

A theme or a type module may provide components for one type, one attribute or one mapped section, resolved before the generic ones:

| Name | Receives | Rendered for |
|---|---|---|
| `EntityPage@<type>` | the `EntityPage` props | every entity of that type, instead of `EntityPage` |
| `Attribute@<name>` | `{ entity, attribute }` (`AttributeProps`) | the values of that attribute, in the panels, instead of the plain values |
| `Section@<key>` | `{ entity, section }` (`SectionProps`) | the section of the note the profile maps under that key, instead of the plain `<section>` |

A theme names them under `components` next to the slots (`components: { "EntityPage@runbook": "./runbook.js", "Attribute@steps": "./steps.js" }`), and they apply to every type: an `Attribute@date` of a theme renders the `date` of a decision and of a meeting alike. A [type module](adding-a-type.md) ships them under `components/`, one file each (`EntityPage.js`, `Attribute@steps.js`, `Section@steps.js`), and they apply to its own type only. The priority is fixed: the project theme (the `components` of the plugin themes, the last declared winning), then the type module, then the default theme; so `EntityPage@runbook` of a theme beats the `EntityPage` of the runbook module, which beats a theme's generic `EntityPage`, which beats the default page. `resolveTheme` files them under `typed` (`pages` by type, `parts` of the themes, `typeParts` by type), `pageComponentFor(theme, type)`, `attributeComponentFor(theme, type, name)` and `sectionComponentFor(theme, type, key)` answer the resolution for a page, and the default `EntityPage` calls the parts through `useAttributePart` and `useSectionPart`, so that a dedicated page component composes them the same way. Every resolved component is listed with the overrides, as `EntityPage@runbook` from `plugin <name>, theme <name>` or from `plugin <name>, theme type module` (`types_dir` for a module of the project). A name that is neither a slot nor one of these three forms is a `ThemeResolutionError`.

The runbook module of the example plugin under `fixtures/plugins/example/types/runbook` ships an `EntityPage.js`, a page that lays the trigger and the owner out as a lead, marks the steps section and lists the other attributes; the site and command line tests render it, and the gallery shows it.

### The contract viewer

The page of an `api` entity whose contract was imported is the API page: the same slot, rendered by `ApiPage` instead of the generic template, in the DOM as on screen. Under the article, a `<section class="api-operations">` holds the operations table (`.api-table`, its column headers visually hidden): one `.api-row` per operation the import attached to a note, in the order of the contract (the names its record keeps), with the method as a chip (`.api-method`, upper case, `DELETE` and `OPTIONS` abbreviated in an `<abbr>` carrying the full name), the path in a `<code>`, the title of the note linked (`a.api-operation`) and how many pages cite it; then the gap rows, `.api-gap` in italics on the soft surface: `.api-gap-without-page` for an operation the contract declares that no note describes, its contract title unlinked and the note "present in the contract, without a page", and `.api-gap-not-in-contract` for an operation note the contract does not declare (the notes `W-OPERATION-UNMATCHED` reports), linked, a hollow chip (`.api-method-none`) and "unknown path" when the note declares none, and the note "described, absent from the contract". The lead under the heading says how the rows were matched and, when gaps exist, what the italics mean. Then a `<section class="contract">` holds the contract block, a `.contract-card`: the `.contract-meta` line on the soft surface with the format (`.contract-format`, `openapi 3.1` or `wsdl 1.1` as the record names it), the file (`.contract-file`) and, at its end, the last change of the contract file worded relative to the build in a `<time>` (`.contract-imported`; the import instant for a contract fetched from a URL); under it the `.contract-body` with the `contract-viewer` island and the note that no schema is copied into the text; at the foot the `.contract-foot` line with the "Download the contract" link (`.contract-download`, the declared URL, or the copy the build placed next to the page under the file name the note points at). The panel of the page cuts the properties to five keys (`apiKeys`: the highlighted properties first, then the declared ones) with a note saying so, and hands the related pages the `endpoint` lead type, so that the operations come first, the note under the list replaced by "On an interface the operations rise to the top: that is the grain we work at." (`api.operationsFirst`). The tree of the space lists the operations under the current API, in the same order as the table. The markdown of the note is untouched: the contract is displayed next to it, never copied into it.

Served, the island holds one link, "Contract data (JSON)", to `fragments/<api id>.contract.json`, the view the build wrote from the cached contract. As soon as it mounts it fetches the fragment, once, "Loading the contract…" standing in the meantime, and opens in the page without any button, as the document viewer opens on the document page; the page never embeds the view and the bundle fetches nothing else. Loaded, the viewer renders an operation list (`.contract-operation-list`, one disclosure button per operation carrying `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` towards its details: the parameters table, the request type, the responses table) and a schema explorer (`.contract-schema-list`, one `aria-pressed` button per schema, the selected one shown with its fields, type, requirement and description). When the fetch fails, over `file://` typically, the link comes back with a sentence saying so. The same component renders an HTTP contract (method and path) and a SOAP one (operation, port and binding): it reads the common view, not the format. It contains no form and no "Try it out": the viewer shows a contract, it never calls the API it describes. The gallery states `entity-page-contract.html` and `api-page-corporate.html` show the page as served, the second with the gaps and the corporate chrome.

In the text, three marks tell three degrees of curation apart, and a fourth style does not exist. An anchor the author wrote whose target is a page of the site carries `class="written"` and is drawn with a solid underline in the accent: someone wrote the link. An anchor the build added around a word the occurrence scan recognised, whose entity has a note, carries `class="recognised"`, links to the page of that entity and is drawn with a dotted underline in the accent. An anchor the build added around a recurring expression that has a keyword page and no note carries `class="recognised-keyword"`, links to the keyword page and is drawn with dashes in `--color-label`, never the accent: the expression only has occurrences. The three classes are exported as `WRITTEN_CLASS`, `RECOGNISED_CLASS` and `RECOGNISED_KEYWORD_CLASS`. An expression is marked only when it has a page, that is above the publication threshold `inference.keyword_pages`; below it nothing is marked, so that one setting governs the volume of the site and the density of the text. Within a page a word is marked on its first occurrence only: a page a written link on the same line or above, or a recognised word above, already leads to stays plain text; marking is rendering, and the mentions and counts of the model do not change. Where two expressions overlap ("build summary" and "build"), the longest wins its position and the words it holds are left out. The dots and dashes never carry the information alone: every anchor the build added carries a `title`, "note: <title>" on a word with a note and "N passages, no note" on an expression without one (`entity.markNote`, `entity.markNoNote`, worded in the site language when the fragments are written), repeated as visually hidden text inside the anchor for assistive technology. The marks come from the fragment, so an override of the slot inherits them by inserting the section HTML as is; the legend is the component's, three entries in the order of the marks (`entity.legendWritten`, `entity.legendRecognised`, `entity.legendKeyword`, "written link", "recognised word, existing note", "recognised word, no note"), and an override that drops it leaves the three styles unexplained. A recognised word inside a written link, split by inline markup (`*entity* page`) or read in a heading that the template renders as text is left unmarked; a word several entities share, a homonym, links to the first of them in identifier order; the page's own name is never linked to itself. Images are `<img>` elements whose `src` is relative to the page for a file of the sources, copied by the build, and the URL as written for an external image, never fetched. An image of the sources that stands on a line of its own comes as a `<figure class="figure">`: the `<img>`, then a `<figcaption>` with the alternative text in a `<span class="figure-caption">` when there is one and the path of the file in its repository in a `<code class="figure-path">`; the default page sets a `<span class="figure-note">` before the path, "Image of the repository, shown in the flow of the text" (`entity.imageNote`, `EntityPageLabels.imageNote`), through `withImageNotes`, so that the fragment carries no sentence of the site language. An image among text or an external image stays a bare `<img>`. A table of the note keeps the markup the markdown gives it, `<thead>` and `<tbody>`; the default stylesheet draws it as a card, the header row in uppercase labels on the soft surface, the first column in the ink, the other cells in the secondary colour.

### The category list

The list of a folder of a space keeps the shell of the entity page: `<div class="category">` holding the same `<nav class="space">` on the left (an `<a class="space-head space-head-link">` to the page of the space heading the tree, the folders of the space with their counts, those on the way to the list open, the folder of the list marked `space-current` with `aria-current="page"`, the other folders linking to their lists) and a `<div class="category-main">` in the centre with the same `<nav class="breadcrumbs">` ("Spaces › space › folders", every folder on the way linked to its list), a `<header class="category-header">` with the `<h1>` and the `<p class="category-lead">`, then a `<div class="category-toolbar">` of two `<details class="category-select">` selectors (`.category-filter` for the highlighted attribute, present only when a value exists to choose, and `.category-sort`), each a `<summary>` reading what is selected with a `▾` mark and a `<ul class="category-choices">` whose entries are links to the pre-rendered variants, the current one a `<span aria-current="true">`, or buttons with `aria-pressed` once the island applies the choices in place; then the `<div class="category-card">` with the `<table class="category-table">` (`th.category-title` with the title linked, `td.category-value`, `td.category-summary`, `td.category-links`), the `<p class="category-foot">` with the rows shown in a `role="status"` span and the note on the links column, and a `<nav class="category-pages">` of page links when the list has several pages. The variants of a list live under `<source>/<folder>/-/`, a folder no identifier can name, so that they never collide with a note.

### The keyword page

The page of a word nobody defined is the entity page without what a note would bring, so that a reader meets the same shell: `<div class="entity entity-with-space keyword">` when the word is filed in a space (`space`, the tree of the glossary source when the configuration names one, else of the source of the first passage, the word inserted as the current page in the folder of its first passage of that space, at the root when the space holds none, at its alphabetical place among the notes, its passage count after its name as a folder shows its count (`passages` on the node, `.count` in the `aria-current` span); `<div class="entity keyword">` without one), the same `<nav class="space">` on the left, the same `<nav class="breadcrumbs">` ("Space › Terms › word"), then a `<header class="entity-header">` with the `<h1 class="keyword-title">`, dotted underneath the way a noteless word is drawn on the map and in the results, and in `.entity-badge` the dashed chip `.badge-noteless` (`labels.noDefinition`, "No definition") followed, when the build found a git date among the citing files, by `<time class="keyword-since">` ("Used since March 2026", `usedSince`). There is no `<article class="entity-body">`, no properties block and no `<footer class="entity-footer">`: a keyword page has no text, no declared property and no file of its own. In their place, an `<aside class="keyword-notice" role="note">` holds the notice (`banner.text`, "Nobody has written a definition, but 17 passages use this word.", from `keyword.noticeLead`; `banner.detail`, what the page is built from and what happens when a note is written, from `keyword.noticeDetail`) and the lead `banner.createNote` as a button, "Propose a definition", a link to the new-file page of the first glossary source on GitHub or GitLab when the build knows one, else to `project.contribute_url`, and nothing when neither is known; then a `<section class="keyword-body">` headed "The passages, in corpus order" with the summary sentence (`summary`, "6 files.") and the passages grouped by file in the order received, each group a `<section class="passage-group">` whose `<h3 class="passage-file">` carries the type chip of the citing page (`typeLabel`), its title linking to it (`title`, the file label when absent) and its count, and whose items link to the position of the passage (`<a class="passage-at">`: `location`, the timecode of a transcript cue, "p. 12" for a page of a converted document, "slide 3", else "line 12", as the build words it from the fragment of the citing page; the theme falls back to its own "line" label when absent) and quote the context in a `<q class="passage-text">`, the expression wrapped in `<mark>` where the context holds it as written (`text`). The panel column stacks the same `.panel-block` disclosures as the entity page: "What we know" (`keyword-facts`: occurrences, files and the names of the spaces the passages come from, `spaces`, as an attribute list, then the note that the word has no declared property because it has no file), "Maybe the same thing" (`keyword-similar`, only when there are any: the similar expressions, one per page whatever the forms it was met under, its title with the other forms as a muted line under it (`aliases`, `.similar-aliases`) and how many passages use it (`count`: the occurrences of a keyword page, the passages that mention a note), under `similarLead`, a wording from the catalogue, `keyword.similarLead`, that asserts no relation), then the related pages through `useSlot`, the pages where the word is used, built from the passages of its fragment, their order note replaced by `keyword.relatedNote`, "Ordered by number of passages. None is “cited”: this word has no note to carry links.", and the neighbourhood folded behind its line, as on the entity page; a keyword page has no block of accompanying words, its map carries them. A test renders the two pages with the same space, neighbourhood and mentions and checks that their markup differs only by these parts.

The build gives the page its data: the counts from the entity, the passages and the leads from its fragment (`passages`, `leads`), the neighbourhood from the `neighbours` block of the model (`keywordNeighbourhoodOf`): a word nobody defined carries no link, so its map is drawn from its co-occurrences, the most frequent first and six at most, an entity of the model as a full node with its type and glyph, another noteless word as a dashed node, each weighed by the number of paragraphs it shares with the word, a neighbour the model no longer holds left out and the total counting every one it still holds; passages come in corpus order, sources in the order the build block declares them, then paths, then lines.

When a note is written later for an expression that had a page, the address of the page survives: the build lists on the fragment of the note the keyword identifiers it took over (`keywords`), and the site writes at each of them a page that forwards to the note, a `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` in the head (`head.redirect` of the `Shell` props, `redirect` of `renderDocument`) and, in the body, the title and a link, worded from `keyword.noteWritten`. The site summary counts them (`redirects:`).

## White label

Everything a reader sees of the organisation comes from `theme.yaml`, validated against [`theme.schema.json`](../../packages/core/schemas/theme.schema.json); `loadTheme(fileSystem, path)` in `@concordance-wiki/site` reads it, reports a faulty key by its path the way `validate-config` does (`light.accent: value does not match the expected format`), resolves every path against the file and returns a `ResolvedThemeConfig` that the renderer consumes through `chromeOf` and `writeThemeAssets`. The build reads the file `project.theme` names in `concordance.yaml`, or `theme.yaml` next to it; a plugin theme ships one as its `tokens`. The [configuration guide](configuration.md#themeyaml) lists every key; this is what each one changes in the site.

| Key | In the site |
|---|---|
| `name` | the site title in the header, linking home, and the suffix of every `<title>` |
| `logo` | before the name in the header. An SVG is inlined, so that `currentColor` and `var(--color-accent)` inside it follow the theme; it is marked `aria-hidden` because the name follows it as text. Any other image is copied under `assets/` and linked with an empty `alt`, for the same reason |
| `favicon` | copied under `assets/` and linked with `<link rel="icon">`, the type inferred from the extension |
| `font` | the families of `--font-display`, `--font-ui` and `--font-mono`, each followed by the families the default theme ships (Instrument Sans for the text, IBM Plex Mono for paths and identifiers, self-hosted under `assets/fonts/` with their licence) then the platform fallbacks; `--font-display` is the text family unless the theme names one. A name is not a download: the project ships its own font files under `assets` and binds them with `@font-face` rules in its `stylesheet`; the site emits no request to any other host, and a test checks that no page or stylesheet references one |
| `radius` | `--radius`, in pixels, with `--radius-small` (half) and `--radius-large` (one and a half); corners of panels, fields and badges derive from them, from 4 to 14 px with the default of 8 |
| `light`, `dark` | the colours of each scheme: the six required, `--color-bg`, `--color-surface`, `--color-border`, `--color-ink`, `--color-muted`, `--color-accent`, and three optional, `--color-label` (the lightest text, `muted` when absent), `--color-soft` (fields, chips and the current entry of the tree, `bg` when absent) and `--color-highlight` (a marked passage, `border` when absent); body, muted and label text must reach 4.5:1 over `bg`, `surface` and `soft`, headings 3:1, ink over `highlight` 4.5:1. The dark palette is a second palette, measured on its own, never an inversion of the light one |
| `default_mode` | which palette the root carries: `system` (default) follows `prefers-color-scheme`, `light` or `dark` starts there; the reader's own choice wins in every case |
| `footer.text`, `footer.links` | a paragraph and a list of links in the second column of the footer, "Declared by the organisation", after the legal pages of the configuration; the column appears only when something was declared |
| `footer.credit` | `true` names the tool and links it to its repository inside the sentence of the generator, "Built with Concordance, a static site generator under the GNU GPL v3 or later licence."; `false`, the default, leaves the generator unnamed. Nothing else in the interface names the tool: a project whose name is its own carries no visible mention of it, and a test renders the gallery with the white-label fixture and asserts it. Technical identifiers stay (the `concordance-island` element, the storage key of the mode switch): a reader never sees them |
| `stylesheet` | copied under `assets/project.css`, wrapped in the `project` layer, and linked after the tool's own stylesheet on every page |
| `assets` | a folder copied as-is under `assets/`, for fonts and icons the stylesheet references by relative URL |
| `labels` | the message overrides of the [configuration guide](configuration.md#labels) |

The fixture under `fixtures/plugins/theme-white-label` is a complete example: another name, an inline SVG logo, a blue palette, a radius of 2, a stylesheet with `@font-face` rules bound to local fonts and an icons folder, `credit: false`. `concordance gallery --theme ./fixtures/plugins/theme-white-label/index.mjs` renders every slot with it.

### The meeting page

The page of a `meeting`, a working session that often exists as a note, a transcript and a deck, stands on the same shell, and the default `EntityPage` hands it to the meeting template as soon as the view model carries `meeting` (a theme or the type module may still provide `EntityPage@meeting`): `<div class="entity entity-with-space meeting">`, the same `<nav class="space">`, the same `<nav class="breadcrumbs">` and `<header class="entity-header">`. Under the title, `.entity-badge` reads the type chip, the duration (`meeting.duration`, the `duration` attribute as written, else the timecode of the last cue of the transcript worded "1 h 12" or "48 min") and the participants (`meeting.participants`: "Pseudonymised participants" when `privacy.pseudonymize.enabled` is set, else "3 participants" from the `participants` attribute). The transcript is an `<ol class="transcript">` of `<li class="cue">`, each at the anchor of its position (`L1`, or `L1-2` for the second document of the page, what the mentions cite), with the timecode as `<a class="cue-time">` (under the hour, minutes and seconds), the speaker in `<b class="cue-speaker">` when the cue names one, then the text; under the last cue a decision was recognised in, `<aside class="meeting-decision" role="note">` names it (`labels.decision`, "Decision taken here"), the decisions no cue names standing at the head of the transcript, after the tabs for a meeting without one, the callout absent when the model links no decision; the file to download follows the lines, then, when pseudonymisation applied, `<p class="meeting-pseudonyms">` (`labels.pseudonymNote`). After the panels, the `<footer class="entity-footer">` with every file. The panel stacks `.meeting-properties` (Date, Duration, Space linking to the file tree of the home page, Files with "3 grouped" and, under the list, why: the signals of the twin-resource reconciliation recorded in the duplicates block of the model for the pairs naming the note, "same folder, same base name, same commit, high textual overlap", else the criterion the merge recorded on the entity), the related pages through `useSlot`, the pages the passages of the meeting evoke with the pages citing it (see [the mentions panel](#mentions-panel)), with their order note replaced by `meeting.relatedNote`, "A meeting does not enter the model: it brings passages, and sometimes a decision someone took the trouble to write elsewhere.", and the neighbourhood folded behind its line. The gallery shows the page as `meeting-page-corporate`, on a working session of the fixtures corpus.

### Modes

`color-scheme: light dark` and the `prefers-color-scheme` query follow the system preference. The header carries a mode switch, a 40 px square button (`.mode-switch`) that toggles the scheme: it draws the glyph of the scheme it switches to (☾ over a light page, ☀ over a dark one, in `.mode-switch-glyph`), is named "Dark mode" in the language of the site for assistive technology alone (`title` and a visually hidden text, `site.darkMode` in the catalogue) and is pressed (`aria-pressed="true"`) while the dark scheme is displayed. The switch reads the scheme in force from `--scheme` on the root, whatever decided it, and hears the system preference change while no choice is stored. Pressing it stores the other scheme in `localStorage` under `concordance-mode` and applies it as `data-mode="light"` or `data-mode="dark"` on the root element, which the `tokens` layer answers with the matching palette and `color-scheme`, so that form controls and scrollbars follow; when the theme's default and the system preference already give the scheme asked for, nothing is stored and the page follows the system again. The switch is served hidden and revealed by its script: without JavaScript, `default_mode` and the system preference apply and no dead control is shown.

To apply a remembered choice before the first paint, every page carries one inline script in its head, before the stylesheets: `MODE_SCRIPT` in `@concordance-wiki/site`, a constant under 300 bytes that reads the key and sets the attribute inside a `try`. It is the only inline script the site emits and never changes from one build to the next, so a content security policy can allow it by hash. The switch itself is the `mode-switch` island: served hidden, it is revealed and wired by a bundle of a few hundred bytes without any framework; without JavaScript, no dead control shows and the theme's default and the system preference apply.

### The accent carries no information on its own

`--color-accent` marks links and the current position, never a status or a decoration, and each of its uses carries a cue that is not a colour: links, the clearing of the type filter of the related pages and the buttons of the contract viewer are underlined; the focus ring is an offset outline; a link written in a note is an underlined anchor in the accent while a recognised word is underlined with dots in the ink colour, and the legend says so in words; the current page of the tree of the space carries a rule on its side and the bold weight; the "cited" mark of a related page is a word; the banner of a keyword page is text; the mode switch names the current scheme. A test lists the rules of the default stylesheets that use the accent and checks the cue of each one, so that a new use has to be added to the list with its cue. A theme author keeps the rule: whatever the accent means in a component, the same meaning is readable without it.

## Overriding a slot from a plugin

A plugin's `theme` contribution names, under `components`, the slot and the path of a module whose default export is a Preact component receiving the props above. Slots the theme leaves out come from the default theme; when several plugins override the same slot, the last one declared in `concordance.yaml` wins. The build summary lists every overridden slot with the plugin and theme that provide it.

```js
export default definePlugin({
  name: "@example/plugin-theme-corporate",
  version: "0.1.0",
  apiVersion: "1",
  contributes: {
    themes: [{ name: "corporate", tokens: "./theme.yaml", components: { Footer: "./footer.js" } }],
  },
});
```

```js
// footer.js
import { h } from "preact";

export default function Footer({ version, generatedAt }) {
  return h("footer", { class: "site-footer" }, `version ${version}, built on ${generatedAt}`);
}
```

A component may be written in TSX and compiled by the plugin; the site only needs a function. The same map names the components of one type, attribute or section (`EntityPage@runbook`, `Attribute@steps`, `Section@steps`), resolved as [rendering per type](#rendering-per-type) says. The default components are semantic HTML without inline styles: landmarks (`header`, `nav`, `main`, `aside`, `footer`), one `h1` per page and headings in order, a skip link to `#main`, `lang` and `dir` on the document. An override keeps those properties so that the accessibility audit still passes. The fixture under `fixtures/plugins/theme-example` overrides the footer and is rendered by the site tests; the [plugins guide](plugins.md) shows the manifest.

The `tokens` of a theme contribution is a `theme.yaml` like the project's; `resolveTheme` loads the one of the last theme when its loader can locate the packages (`rootOf`), and its stylesheet and assets come with it: `stylesheet` and `assets` of the contribution are read relative to the package, the `stylesheet` and `assets` the file itself names relative to the file, the file's stylesheet winning when both name one and both asset folders being copied. The project's own `theme.yaml` wins over any plugin's.

### The document page

An entity that is a document, a deck, a PDF or a transcript, or a note merged with one, keeps the same template: what its files add comes after the note's sections, inside the article, one `<section class="document document-<unit>">` per file in path order, headed by `Document <code>file name</code>`. Its anatomy, top to bottom:

- `<p class="document-files">`: the download link of the original file (`<a class="document-download" download>`) and, when the build kept a PDF, `<a class="document-pdf">` to open it as the browser does;
- the island `document-viewer`, only when the build produced the viewer bundles and the document has a PDF: a `<button class="document-open" hidden>` and an empty `<div class="document-viewer" hidden>`, both served inert. The island's script reveals the button; the first click imports the viewer bundle, `viewer-pdf-<hash>.js`, which no page loads on its own, and opens the PDF in the container with pdf.js: a toolbar (a counter such as `slide 3 / 12`, `Zoom out` and `Zoom in` around the percentage, a `Find in the document` field over the extracted text that jumps to the first matching position and counts the others in a `role="status"` line) above a `<canvas class="viewer-page">`; the pages are reached through the rail, which drives the viewer once it is open. The button then toggles the viewer (`aria-expanded`). When the import fails, as it does over `file://` in browsers that refuse module scripts from the disk, the container shows a `role="status"` notice with a link to the PDF instead;
- `<nav class="document-rail" aria-label="Pages | Slides | Cues">`: one entry per position, its label (`slide 3`, `00:12:05`) and the first line of its text as a caption, linking to the text block of the position; once the viewer runs, an entry also shows that page in it, and the entry of the page shown carries `class="current"` and `aria-current`. Image thumbnails will replace the captions when the converter produces them;
- `<div class="document-text">`: one `<details id="L<n>">` per position, its label as the `<summary>` and its text inside, the first one open. The ids are the anchors the mentions of the page cite (`…/index.html#L3`); a second document on the same page suffixes its rank (`L3-2`) so that ids stay unique. A document without extracted text says so in a `<p class="empty">`.

Without JavaScript, the download links, the PDF link, the rail and the text are all there and every link works; only the viewer is missing. The strings of the viewer travel in the island props (`labels`), so that no visible string lives in a bundle.

The page of an office document, a deck, a report, a spreadsheet or a PDF, alone or merged with its note, is laid out differently: the build hands `EntityPageProps.document` (`documentPageOf`: the kind of the original file from its extension, the page count, the size, the date read from the file else from the repository, the author, the files that make the document and the labels of the page) and the `EntityPage` slot renders `DocumentPage` instead of the generic page. A page with a transcript among its documents, or with notes alone, never gets the block. The build reads a converted file and the PDF kept next to it in the sources as one document (`documentsOf`): the office file is the original, the PDF its preview, so that the kind reads "Presentation" rather than "PDF", the download gives the `.pptx` and the files list each format once; a page count or a size the original does not state is read from the preview, and the properties say so (`document.previewNote`, `DocumentPageView.fromPreview`). Its anatomy: `<div class="entity entity-with-space document-page">`, the same `<nav class="space">` (folded by year and month when every page of the space is dated, as for a meeting), the same breadcrumb through the month, the `<header class="entity-header">` with the title and the `.document-line` (the kind as the `.badge`, then `.document-pages`, `.document-size` and the `<time class="document-date">`); then the three views behind the [tabs](#tabs) of the theme, `<div class="tabs document-views">`, the panels `document-view`, `document-text` and `document-notes`, with the `.document-download` link, carrying the `download` attribute, at the end of the bar. The document view holds the `.document-stage`: the `<nav class="document-rail">` of numbered thumbnails (`.document-number`, the label of the position for assistive technology, the first one `current`), then the `.document-render`: the `document-viewer` island served with `open: true`, which imports the viewer as soon as its panel is shown and never shows its button, the PDF as the browser shows it (`<object class="document-embed">` with the link to the PDF as its fallback) behind a `<noscript>` when the island exists and in the page otherwise, and the `.document-render-note` with the two notes. The text view holds the same `<details id="L<n>">` blocks as the generic page in a `.document-text`; the notes view the sections of the note with their legend, or `<p class="empty">` when there is none. The panel stacks the properties (`#entity-properties`: type, author, pages, date, every row kept when the corpus has no datum, the `.panel-note` on the date only when it was read from the file, another when the size or the count comes from the preview), the files (`#document-files`, `<ul class="document-twins">`, one `.document-twin-name` and `.document-twin-role` per file), the related pages, the pages the passages of the document evoke with the pages citing it, under the note `document.relatedNote`, "A document does not enter the model: its pages bring passages, and the note that describes it stands among its files.", and the neighbourhood folded. The toolbar the viewer builds is the same on both pages: `.viewer-counter` (the unit visually hidden), `.viewer-zoom-out`, `.viewer-zoom` and `.viewer-zoom-in`, the `.viewer-find` field with its glyph and its placeholder, `.viewer-status`, and the `<canvas class="viewer-page">` on its `.viewer-stage`; the strip drives the pages, an entry showing its page in the viewer once it is open rather than leading to its text. The gallery state `document-page-corporate.html` shows the page as served.

## Islands

Only interactive components are hydrated. An island is created with `island(name, Component)`: the server renders the component's markup inside `<concordance-island data-island="name" data-props="…">`, the props serialised as JSON and escaped like any attribute. A hydration entry per island reads the props back and mounts the same component with `hydrate`; `bundleIslands` produces one minified script per entry with esbuild, a classic script unless the entry is declared `module`, named `<name>-<hash>.js` after its content, so that two builds give the same bytes. Every island of a page is a classic script because some browsers refuse a module script on a page opened from the disk, and the site promises to work there; only the viewer bundles, imported on demand and never by a page, are modules. A page loads only the bundles of the islands it contains, through `<script defer>` (`head.classicScripts` of the `Shell`), or `<link rel="modulepreload">` and `<script type="module" defer>` for a bundle declared `module`; apart from the mode script of the head, a page carries no other script.

The default theme has nine islands. The mentions panel, described under [Mentions panel](#mentions-panel), whose body is served as static markup and gains its controls once it mounts. The mode switch of the header, described under [Modes](#modes), the trail beside it, described under [Trail](#trail), the table of contents of the panel, described under [Table of contents](#table-of-contents), and the tabs of the meeting and document pages, described under [Tabs](#tabs), whose entries are plain JavaScript and load no framework. The document viewer, described under [The document page](#the-document-page), whose entry is plain JavaScript of under two kilobytes and imports the viewer bundle on demand. The search, `search`, which wraps the field of the header on every page and the results on the results page: it loads the index files under `search/` by injecting script tags, so that it works from a `file://` page, draws the best results under the field in an element of class `search-suggestions` and the whole list on the results page with the default `SearchResults` component, gives the field the `/` and `Escape` shortcuts and shows the `.search-clear` button, served hidden in the form, while the field holds something, a click emptying the field and rewriting the address at once; on the results page it also draws the facets from the entity table, counts them in the browser over the results of the query, filters the list by the selected values and recalls them above it, every box of a facet, chip of an active filter or link of the closest form carrying the address of the state it leads to and followed in place through `onNavigate`; it draws the rows twenty at a time (`RESULTS_BATCH`), the `.results-more` button under them adding the next batch in place, any change of state starting again from the first, and, the query and the filters living in the address so that a shared link replays the search, pushes every facet followed to the history, rewrites the current entry as the reader types, replays the state on `popstate` and restores the scroll position remembered per address. A theme restyles the results through the `.results`, `.result`, `.result-lead` (the first row, expanded), `.result-head`, `.result-title`, `.result-cited` and `.result-cited-count` (the bare count of a condensed row, in the monospace family), `.snippet`, `.result-facts`, `.badge` and `.breadcrumb` classes, a word without a note through `.result-keyword` (a dashed card with a dotted title by default, in the suggestions under the field as on the results page), `.result-subtitle` and `.result-detail`, the notes through `.facets-note` and `.results-note`, the facets through `.facets` (a full-height column on the surface colour, ruled on its right, from 700 px), `.facets-fold` and `.facets-head` (the disclosure folding the column where the page is narrow, its heading kept for assistive technology alone where the column stands), `.facet` (one `<details>` per facet, `open` for the primary ones), `.facet-value` with its `.facet-active`, `.facet-disabled` and `.facet-keyword` states (a box, a label and a `.count`), the recalled filters through `.active-filters`, `.active-filter`, `.remove-filter` and `.clear-filters`, the empty state through `.search-closest`; the layout through `.results-layout` (a grid whose first column, 250 px, the facets take from 700 px) and `.results-main`; a theme replacing the `SearchResults` slot is used for the served results page, not for what the island draws afterwards (see the [architecture guide](architecture.md#search-index)). The contract viewer of the API page, described under [The contract viewer](#the-contract-viewer), the only island that fetches data, and only on demand; it is declared as a UI component of the default theme (`defaultThemeManifest()`), the first contribution of that point, and the site bundles the UI components a plugin contributes the same way, after its own. The category list, `category-list`, described under [The category list](#the-category-list), which wraps the table of a list whose sort and filter would take too many pre-rendered pages: the served markup is the first page by title, and the selectors appear once it mounts. Without JavaScript, everything stays readable, every link works and the search field submits to the results page.

The viewer itself is not an island of the page: `viewerIslands()` names two more bundles, `viewer-pdf` (the viewer over the legacy build of pdf.js, about 490 kB) and `viewer-pdf-worker` (the worker of pdf.js, about 1.3 MB, which the viewer points its `workerSrc` at), that `buildSite` bundles only when a fragment carries a PDF preview and that no page references in a `<script>` or a `<link>`: their hashed names travel in the props of the document island, the budget summary announces their size like any island's, and the initial bundles of a page are unchanged whether they exist or not.

## Table of contents

The "On this page" block of the entity page lists the sections of the note with a heading, counted after its heading where the block is folded, as an island `toc` around the `<ol class="toc-list">`. The served markup marks the first entry `aria-current="location"`: at the top of the page that is the section being read, and without any script it stays so. Once its script runs, the island observes the sections the entries point at with an `IntersectionObserver` over the top third of the viewport (`TOC_ROOT_MARGIN`) and moves the mark to the last section that entered it, back to the first entry when none is there; a browser without the observer, or a page whose sections it cannot find, keeps the served mark. The stylesheet draws the current entry with the accent on its left rule, the ink and a heavier weight (`.toc-list a[aria-current]`), the other entries with the border colour.

## Tabs

The meeting page and the document page put their views behind the same `Tabs` component (`packages/site/src/theme/default/tabs.tsx`), which follows the tablist pattern: a `<div class="tabs …">` holding a `.tabs-bar` (the `<div class="tabs-list" role="tablist">` named by the page, one `<a class="tab" role="tab">` per view, `href="#<panel>"`, `aria-controls` naming its panel, `aria-selected` true on the first one, and whatever the page adds at the end of the bar), then `.tabs-panels` with one `<section class="tabs-panel" role="tabpanel" id="<panel>">` per tab, labelled by it. Served that way, the anchors work without any script and the stylesheet (`chrome/tabs.css`) shows one panel at a time, the targeted one, the one holding the targeted anchor, else the first, marking its tab by a rule and the bold weight; a browser without `:has()` shows the panels one under the other. The `tabs` island, a few hundred bytes without any framework (`wireTabs`), takes over once it runs, marking the block `tabs-scripted`: the panel shown is the one the address names or the one holding the anchor it names, else the first; a tab followed shows its panel, moves the selected state and the tab order to it (`tabindex`) and writes its id in the address without scrolling (`history.replaceState`); the arrow keys move the selection along the row, Home and End to its ends; a fragment naming an anchor inside a panel, followed from the page, shows that panel and scrolls to the anchor. A panel shown receives the `concordance:tabshow` event (`TAB_SHOWN_EVENT`): a document viewer served `open` in a panel of tabs opens on it rather than at once, so that only the visible panel opens its viewer.

## Trail

The `trail` island sits in the bar, before the mode switch, on every page: the pages the reader visited, in order, each a link, folded behind a square button, so that an investigation can be resumed or shared without a line of its own under the bar. It is served empty, because what it shows lives in the URL and in the reader's browser; the stylesheet hides an empty island, so that without JavaScript, or while the trail holds no page, the bar shows no button. The header receives `trail: { base, current?, labels }`: `base` is the prefix from the page to the site root (`../../` for `glossary/source/index.html`), `current` the identifier and title of the entity of the page (absent on the home, index and to-do pages, which carry the trail without entering it) and `labels` the messages `trail.title`, `trail.pin`, `trail.unpin` and `trail.earlier` of the catalogue. A theme that overrides `Header` decides whether to render `Trail`; a header that receives no `trail` renders the island with the default English labels and records no page.

Once its script runs and the trail holds a page, the island renders a `<details class="trail-fold">` whose summary is the button of the bar (`.trail-button`, a 40 px square drawing a bookmark, `trail.title` as its visually hidden text and its `title`), and under it, unfolded as a popover under the bar, `<nav class="trail" aria-label="Trail">` holding an `<ol class="trail-list">` of links to `<base><id>/index.html`, the current page marked `aria-current="page"`, and a `<button class="trail-pin" aria-pressed>`. The current page is appended to the trail read at load, once: reloading a page does not repeat it. Beyond twelve entries (`TRAIL_SHOWN_MAX`), the oldest fold into one `<details class="trail-earlier">` whose summary reads "… N earlier pages" and which lists them when opened; beyond fifty (`TRAIL_KEPT_MAX`), the oldest are dropped. An empty trail leaves the island empty.

The trail travels in the URL fragment as `#trail=<id>,<id>,…`, each identifier passed through `encodeURIComponent`, so that a link to the page shows the same trail to whoever opens it and a reload restores it; the island writes the fragment with `history.replaceState` when the trail changes, and makes every internal link of the page carry it when followed, by appending the fragment to the link's `href` at click time (same protocol and host, no fragment of its own, no `download`). A link to a passage keeps its own fragment: the trail of the tab is also kept in `sessionStorage` under `concordance-trail`, and a page without a fragment trail reads it from there. The fragment is read first, then the tab, then the pinned trail; nothing is fetched, so the trail works over `file://`.

Pinning stores the trail, titles included, in `localStorage` under `concordance-trail`; the button is pressed while the trail shown is the pinned one, and every page the reader then visits is added to the stored trail. A new tab, or a later visit, without a fragment trail starts from the pinned trail. Unpinning removes the key. A fragment trail that differs from the pinned one is shown unpressed and leaves the stored one alone until the reader pins it. A storage that cannot be read or written is treated as empty and the trail holds for the page and its URL.

## Mentions panel

The `MentionsPanel` slot is the "Related pages" block of the right panel: it answers one question, which pages evoke this entity, and whether a person wrote a link or the tool recognised the words. Its anatomy, top to bottom:

- an `<aside class="mentions panel-block">` holding a `<details class="panel-fold">` whose summary is the heading, "Related pages" (`related.title`) with the number of related pages;
- the island, `mentions-panel`, holding an `<ol class="related-list">` with one `<li class="related-page">` per page that evokes the entity, ordered by number of passages, written links (provenance `explicit_link` or `frontmatter_ref`) and recognised mentions (`section_mention`, `glossary_occurrence`) counted alike, the corpus order breaking ties: the title of the page as a link (`.related-title`), its type (`.related-type`), its passage count (`.related-count`, the unit for assistive technologies only), then one excerpt (`.related-excerpt`) linking to the passage, prefixed "Cited ·" (`.related-mark`, `related.cited`) when the page writes a link to the entity, in which case the excerpt is the sentence of the citing note around that link, and by the page, slide or timecode when the passage was read from a document; the words naming the entity are wrapped in `<mark>` when the build found them; six entries stand in view: the first three in the list, the next three in a `<details class="related-others">` whose summary counts every page beyond the three ("N others", `related.others`), closed only where the stylesheet condenses the panel; the served pages beyond six stand in a `<details class="related-beyond">` whose summary is worded as the button ("Show the N others", `related.showOthers`), so that a reader without JavaScript reaches them; the note under the list (`.related-note`, `related.orderNote`, "Ordered by number of passages, written and recognised together. “Cited” marks a link present in the text.") says how the entries are ordered, or, on a keyword page, a meeting, a document page or an API page, how that page relates to the model;
- the way to the pages beyond the entries in view: a link to the fragment of the entity in the served HTML when some mentions are not held, a "Show the N others" button once the island runs, which lists every page held and obtains the rest; on a phone (`(width < 43.75rem)`, the query of the stylesheet, read once the island mounts) the island lists two entries before the button.

The related pages of a note of the model are the pages that cite it: the provenances of its links located in the citing note, whichever end relation typing put first. A keyword page, a meeting and a document page (an entity whose documents are a deck, a PDF, never a transcript) stand outside the model: nothing links to them. Their panel lists the pages their own passages evoke, from the passages of the keyword fragment or from the provenances read in the files of the page, its note and its documents, each entry counted and quoting its first passage, the excerpt leading to the passage on the page itself (`#L<n>`), joined, for a meeting or a document, to the pages citing it. `relatedViewOf` names the view of an entity, `citing` or `evoked`; `relatedMentionsOf` builds the list, `mentionsOf` the citing view alone and `evokedMentionsOf` the evoked one.

The build writes `fragments/<id>.mentions.json` for every entity with a related page, holding all its mentions with hrefs relative to its page, and never a global index of mentions. The mentions are served in the order of the panel: the first passage of each of the six pages listed first, then the other passages page by page, the pages of the lead type before every other; every passage of a page with several says how many the page holds (`passages`), so that a served slice counts right. What the page carries depends on the number of mentions, three regimes:

| Mentions in all | Served HTML | Once the island runs |
|---|---|---|
| up to `initial` (20, `build.mentions_inline`) | every page of every mention, six in view | the filters, the button listing the others |
| more, under 200 (`MENTIONS_EMBEDDED_MAX`) | the first `initial`, a link to the fragment, and the rest inside a `<script type="application/json" id="mentions-embedded">` block next to the island | a button reveals the embedded rest without a request |
| 200 or more | the first `initial` and a link to the fragment | behind a server, a button fetches the fragment; over `file://`, where a page may not fetch, the link stands once the pages held are listed |

The island adds a `role="group"` of controls named after the block: a search field ("Filter these pages") filtering on the title, the type and the passages without regard to case, and a "Types" disclosure with one labelled checkbox per type among the pages, its count next to it, an `<output>` line, a status region, saying how many pages are shown ("19 of 35 pages", `related.pagesOf`) and a "Clear all" button that ticks every type again. Every control is a native element, labelled, reachable with the keyboard alone; the controls do not exist in the served HTML, so a reader without JavaScript sees the static list and its links. The exported `relatedMentionsOf`, `mentionsOf`, `evokedMentionsOf`, `mentionsPanelOf` and `relatedLabels` build the view model from the model and the catalogue, `groupByPage` and `typeCounts` are what the island groups and counts with; `DEFAULT_MENTIONS_INLINE` is the default threshold, `RELATED_INLINE` (6), `RELATED_CONDENSED` (3) and `RELATED_PHONE` (2) the entries in view. The two messages with a placeholder, `related.pagesOf` and `related.showOthers`, keep their braces quoted in ICU (`'{count}'`) so that the island fills them in the browser.

## Stylesheet

The site ships one stylesheet in four cascade layers, declared first so that their order never depends on the source order:

```css
@layer tokens, base, components, project;
```

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| `tokens` | custom properties generated from `theme.yaml`: `--font-display`, `--font-ui`, `--font-mono`, `--radius`, `--radius-small`, `--radius-large`, `--space-1` to `--space-6`, `--measure`, and, in every palette block, `--scheme` (`light` or `dark`, the palette in force, which the mode switch reads), the nine colours `--color-bg`, `--color-surface`, `--color-soft`, `--color-border`, `--color-ink`, `--color-muted`, `--color-label`, `--color-accent`, `--color-highlight`, and `--shadow-float`, the shadow of what floats over the page, none in the dark scheme |
| `base` | document defaults: typography, links, tables, focus ring, skip link, `prefers-reduced-motion` |
| `components` | one block per slot of the default theme, selected by class names |
| `project` | the content of the project's `stylesheet:`, which wins every cascade by construction |

The `base` and `components` layers are read from `assets/css/` of the site package, one file per concept: the document defaults at the top level (`reset.css`, `typography.css`, `controls.css`, `accessibility.css`), the parts every page shares under `chrome/` (the bar, the search field, the drawer, the space tree, the panel, the breadcrumb, the footer, the widths), and one file per page under `pages/` (`home.css`, `entity-page.css`, `keyword-page.css`, `search-results.css`, `spaces.css`, `space.css`, `category-list.css`, `meeting-page.css`, `api-page.css`, `document-page.css`, the neighbourhood and the mentions among them). `CSS_FILES` in `src/css/stylesheet.ts` lists them per layer in the order the cascade needs; the assembly follows that list, never the file system, and a new page adds its file to the list after the ones it relies on. `siteStylesheet` writes the `@font-face` rules of the shipped families (outside the layers, addressed as `fonts/<file>` next to the stylesheet) and the first three layers to `assets/site.css`; `writeThemeAssets` copies the eight woff2 files and their licence under `assets/fonts/`; `projectStylesheet` wraps the project's file in the fourth layer and writes it to `assets/project.css`, linked after the first on every page. The layer order is declared at the top of the first file, so the project's rules win whatever their specificity and whichever file loads first. `color-scheme: light dark` follows the system preference; the dark palette also applies under `data-mode="dark"` on the root, which the mode switch sets and remembers, and a `default_mode` of `light` or `dark` in `theme.yaml` starts with that palette. Properties are logical (`margin-inline`, `inset-block-start`), so a right-to-left locale needs no second stylesheet. A project overrides anything by writing plain CSS in its stylesheet; a theme plugin's `stylesheet` enters the same layer.

## The default theme

The default theme is a corporate wiki, familiar in thirty seconds: spaces, a tree, a breadcrumb, a title, the text, a table of contents. What distinguishes the site is the right panel, which shows where the subject is evoked elsewhere without anyone having written those references. Four choices hold it together, and the tests hold the theme to them.

**One type family.** Instrument Sans serves the text, the headings included: the hierarchy comes from size and weight, never from a second face. IBM Plex Mono is reserved to file paths and identifiers (the `code`, `pre` and `kbd` elements, the counts). Both ship with the site under `assets/fonts/`, Latin and Latin Extended subsets as woff2 with their OFL licence, bound by the `@font-face` rules of `site.css`; no page calls a font host. Labels are plain language: "Related pages", "Cited", "Something to correct?", from the `related.*` and `entity.*` messages of the catalogue, in English and French.

**A warm light palette, a dark one measured on its own.** The page is `#EFEDE9`, a surface `#FFFFFF`, a rule `#E3E0DA`, the text `#1F2124`, secondary text `#3A3E44`, the lightest labels `#676C74`, the accent `#A8431C`, a marked passage `#FBE3D4`, the soft surfaces `#F7F6F3`; the dark palette is not an inversion but a second set of nine colours whose contrasts the same test measures: the page `#0F1113`, a surface `#181B1E`, the soft surfaces `#22262A`, a rule `#282C31`, the text `#ECEAE6`, secondary text `#A8AEB6`, the lightest labels `#8D939B`, the accent `#E8703A`, raised in lightness from the light `#A8431C` so that a link holds 4.5:1 over every dark ground, a marked passage `#4A2A1B`. The ground goes under the surface in both schemes; in the dark one the hierarchy is carried by the grounds and the rules alone: the `tokens` layer sets `--shadow-float`, the shadow of what floats over the page (the live results, the trail, the menus of the filters, the drawer), to none, where the light scheme casts a soft one. Radii go from 4 to 14 px (`--radius-small`, `--radius`, `--radius-large`). The accent marks links and the current position only, never a status or a decoration, as [the section above](#the-accent-carries-no-information-on-its-own) details.

**No account.** The site is static and public: no avatar, no profile, no share button; the top bar carries the mark and the name, the search field with its `/` shortcut, three links (Spaces, A–Z index, Recent), the mode switch, and no build statistic, the to-do count living in the footer.

**Three widths, mobile first.** The base styles are the phone's and the media queries add the wider layouts. Under 700 px (`43.75rem`) the bar is reduced to a menu button, the mark and the name, cut with an ellipsis where it does not fit, and a search button, a 40 px square drawing the magnifier alone that unfolds the field under the bar; the menu button is a 48 px bordered square, the summary of a `<details>` drawer, so that it opens without any script and over `file://`, and the open drawer takes the whole screen: ✕ and the name in the bar, the search field first, the spaces with their initials badges and page counts, the tree of the current space unfolded to the page, the index and the recent changes at the foot, the mode switch after them. The page reads in one column: the breadcrumb cut to the last folder and the page, the type and the short date under the title, the note, then the foot of the article on one line of 48 px, the name of the file alone (the source and the folders of the path sit in a `<span class="entity-source-folders">` the phone hides) and the short edit label "Edit" (`entity.editShort`, `.entity-edit-short`, swapped by the stylesheet for the long label and the question), without the legend of the marks, then the blocks of the panel as closed disclosures headed in sentence case with their counts, in the order "On this page", "Properties", "Related pages" (open, the titles, counts and excerpts of three pages and the others behind their count) and "Neighbourhood map" with the number of pages the map draws; every target is 48 px. Between 700 and 1099 px the search button reads as a small field after the name, "Search" beside the magnifier, and unfolds the field under itself; the mode switch joins the bar, the tree stays in the drawer, and the panel stands in a narrow column beside the text (`11.625rem`), condensed: the properties show their values alone, the table of contents is left out, the related pages three titles and the others behind their count, and the neighbourhood spans the foot of the page. From 1100 px (`68.75rem`) the entity page is a grid of three regions, `space`, `main` and `side`: the tree of the space in a 16 rem column on the left, the text in the centre, the full panel on the right, the menu button gone and the content of the drawer, the links and the search field, kept in view in the bar. The folding needs no script: the drawer, the tree and every block of the panel are served as `<details>`, and at the widths where the layout has room for them the stylesheet keeps their content in view through `::details-content` (`display: block; content-visibility: visible`), so that a browser without that pseudo-element still shows them folded behind their heading, one click away. Targets are 40 px or more on the desktop and no text goes under 13 px. The gallery state `entity-page-corporate` shows the page on a rule of the fixtures corpus with every region filled, and `entity-page-phone`, `entity-page-drawer` (the drawer served open) and `entity-page-tablet` the same page as the narrow layouts fold it; the gallery renders at one width, so narrow the browser to see them. The state `screen-page-corporate` shows the same template on the entity page screen of the corpus, the most consulted type: the screens folder open on its neighbours, a table of the checks the screen applies drawn as a card, and the sketch it was drawn from as a figure in the flow of the text, captioned with its alternative text, the note and its path. The results page follows the same rule with two regions: from 700 px the facets take a 250 px column on the left of the results, as tall as the page, on the surface colour and ruled on its right, the primary ones open and the secondary ones folded and no heading over them; under it the whole column folds behind its "Filters" heading above the list, through the same `<details>` and `::details-content` mechanism. The gallery state `search-results-corporate` shows it on a query over the fixtures corpus, a space selected and a word without a note among the results.

## Gallery

`concordance gallery [--output dir] [--theme plugin] [--config file]` renders every slot with fixture view models into a static page set, so that a theme is styled and checked without building a corpus. The output folder (`./gallery` by default) receives `index.html`, one page per slot and state, the stylesheet under `assets/site.css` and the island bundles next to it; open `index.html` in a browser. The index groups the states by board of the reference design, in the order of the boards: the home, the entity page, the search results, the accessibility board (no state of its own), the keyword page, the meeting page, the API page, the neighbourhood map open, the phone and the tablet, the A–Z index, the spaces, the page of a space, the category list, the screen page, the document page, the decision page and the dark mode, then the to-do page, the two panels framed under a heading of their own, and the chrome in its other shapes (a right-to-left locale, a header with a logo, a footer with a project text). Every board has its state in the corporate chrome on the fixtures corpus, named after it (`home-corporate`, `entity-page-corporate`, `search-results-corporate`, `keyword-page-corporate`, `meeting-page-corporate`, `api-page-corporate`, `entity-page-map`, `entity-page-phone`, `entity-page-drawer` with the drawer open, `entity-page-tablet`, `index-corporate`, `spaces-corporate`, `space-corporate`, `category-corporate`, `screen-page-corporate`, `document-page-corporate`, `decision-page-corporate`, `entity-page-dark` and `home-dark` with the dark scheme forced on the root, `todo-corporate`), beside the default state, an empty one where meaningful (no mention, no neighbour, no result, nothing to do) and the degraded cases: a corpus fed by one repository (`home-single-source`), a note without a property (`entity-page-no-property`), a keyword page whose transcript passages carry no timecode (`keyword-page-no-timecode`), an API page whose contract could not be fetched (`api-page-no-contract`), and, for every page that carries an island of its own, the page served without the scripts of its islands (`home-no-script`, `entity-page-no-script`, `search-results-no-script`, `keyword-page-no-script`, `meeting-page-no-script`, `api-page-no-script`, `document-page-no-script`, captioned "server HTML only"). Each board carries its caption and a link to the screen note of the demonstration; each state its caption, its width, who renders its slot, and the page framed at the width its board is drawn at, 390 px for a phone, 834 px for a tablet, 1440 px for a desktop (`width` on the page entry, 1440 when absent), the heading of the state opening the page alone. Three buttons above the boards, a classic island served hidden like the mode switch, set every frame to one width once the script runs; without it each frame keeps its own. A section then lists the eleven slots in order and who renders each (the default theme, or the plugin and theme that override it), and a last one every registered type, the core ones, those the plugins contribute and those of the project profile, with one page each (`type-<slug>.html`): the note template of the type rendered as a note of that type, through the generic entity page or, when a theme or the type module provides one, through its `EntityPage@<type>` component, which the index names; a type without a template has no page. Those pages pass the same accessibility checks as the rest. The pages use a neutral palette and the labels of the default theme; the stylesheet follows the system colour scheme, the switch in the header forces one, and setting `data-mode="dark"` on the root element of a page previews the dark palette without JavaScript: the two states of the dark board are served so (`scheme: "dark"` on the page entry, `scheme` on the render options), without the boot script that would apply a remembered choice over them. In the site package, every state is one file under `src/gallery/states/` exporting its page entry (file, slot, state, description, board, width, chrome and props, `scripts: false` for a page served without its island scripts, `scheme: "dark"` for a page served in the dark palette), listed in board order in `src/gallery/pages.ts`, the boards themselves in `src/gallery/boards.ts`; the fixture data lives under `src/gallery/fixtures/`, one file per page or concept (the chrome, the mentions, the neighbourhood, each page, the degraded cases), exported together as `galleryFixtures`, with what several of them share in `fixtures/corpus.ts`. A new page adds its fixtures file, its state file and one line to the list. One test per state pins the skeleton of its page, the elements in document order with their classes, role, ARIA attributes and island name, text and hrefs left out (`skeletonOf`, exported by the site package), as a file snapshot under `packages/site/test/gallery/__snapshots__/<state>.skeleton.html`: a change of structure is reviewed against its board, and the snapshot updated once the change is intended.

The theme comes from the registry the build uses: `--theme` names a plugin package, or the path of its module when it starts with `.` or `/`, and may be repeated, the last one winning a slot; without it, the `plugins:` of `concordance.yaml` (`--config`, or the file in the current directory when it exists) apply, and without any the default theme renders alone. The `theme.yaml` of the project, named by `project.theme` or found next to the configuration, replaces the neutral palette and the fixture chrome with the project's name, logo, favicon, palette, stylesheet, footer and credit; without one, the `tokens` of the last plugin theme do the same, and the summary names the file in use. An invalid theme file stops the command with exit code 1 and one line per faulty key; a named file that does not exist, with exit code 2. A plugin loaded from a path still needs its package resolvable by name, as in a build. The fixtures are exported by `@concordance-wiki/site` as `galleryFixtures` and the page set as `galleryPages`; `buildGallery` writes the pages through any file system and returns a report with every page, its size and its accessibility findings.

The command measures every page against the budget and runs a static accessibility checker on each one, `checkAccessibility` in the site package, with no dependency: the document carries `lang`; exactly one `h1`, heading levels never skipping; every image an `alt`; every form control a label, an `aria-label`, an `aria-labelledby` or a text; a `main` landmark, once, with a `nav`, a `header` and a `footer`; every link a name; unique `id`s; the first focusable element a skip link to an existing fragment; and the four ARIA rules listed under [Accessibility](#accessibility). A page over budget or a finding fails the command with exit code 1 and one line per problem on stderr. The command also runs `checkContrast` on the palette of the stylesheet and prints one `warning: contrast:` line per pair of colours under its minimum ratio; a warning does not fail the command. The site tests run the checker on every gallery page of the default theme, and continuous integration builds the gallery into `reports/gallery` and publishes it as the `gallery` artifact of every run.

## Neighbourhood map

The `Neighbourhood` slot draws a one-hop star map of the entity, deliberately modest: at most twelve nodes, every one of them labelled, and always accompanied by the list that says the same thing in text. This section describes the anatomy of the default map, what its layout guarantees, and how a type is told from another without colour.

**Anatomy.** The slot renders a `<section class="neighbourhood">` with its heading ("Neighbourhood map" and the title of the page), then the controls (`.neighbourhood-controls`: the distance the model records, "Distance · 1 hop", and the type filter, a `<details class="neighbourhood-types">` whose summary reads "Types" and which lists one checkbox per type of the neighbourhood, labelled with the type and its count, served all ticked), then a `<figure class="neighbourhood-map">` holding an `<svg class="neighbourhood-graph">`, the framed map, and a `<figcaption>` (the caption sentence for assistive technology, then the legend, "existing page" beside a plain rule and "word without a note" beside a dashed one), then, when the model holds more neighbours than the map draws, `<p class="neighbourhood-total">` ("N neighbours in total, more than the map shows."), then the head of the list ("The N neighbours · textual equivalent"), the `<ul id="neighbourhood-list">` (one row per neighbour opening on the accent mark: the title linking to the page, the type as plain text in `.neighbour-type`, the relation, the count), and the note "Six neighbours at most, always named. Beyond that the map teaches nothing: the list takes over." Inside the SVG, in drawing order: a `<defs>` sprite with one `<symbol id="glyph-…">` per shape the map uses, one `<line class="map-edge">` per neighbour from the centre to its node, one `<g class="map-node">` per neighbour, and the `<g class="map-centre">`. A node group carries `data-weight` (the co-occurrence count) and `data-glyph` (the glyph name of the type, when it has one), so that a project stylesheet can style nodes by type without touching the markup. The type filter needs no script: the types are ranked in the order they first appear, every edge, node and row of a typed neighbour carries `data-type` with that rank, and the components stylesheet hides the elements of an unticked rank through `:has()`, one selector per rank up to the twelve nodes the map may hold; a browser without `:has()` shows every node, the checkboxes doing nothing. Every node group holds its shape (`.map-shape`), its glyph (`.map-glyph`) and its label (`.map-label`): a `<text>` in plain text right next to the node, never a hover as the only way to read a name. A label longer than 28 characters is cut with an ellipsis, the full title following in a `<title>` child and, always, in the list. The `font-size` is a presentation attribute of the `<svg>` (12 units), because the layout estimates label widths from it: a stylesheet that changes it also changes the estimate's accuracy.

**Layout guarantees.** `layoutNeighbourhood(centre, items, titleOf)` in `@concordance-wiki/site` is a pure function: the centre in the middle, the neighbours on a ring in the order received (rank order, clockwise from the top), the ring radius growing with the number of nodes from 64 to 120 units. Each label sits on the outer side of its node, right of it on the right half, left of it on the left half, above or below it near the poles, and the centre's title sits under the centre. Text widths are estimated at 0.55 em per character in the font size of the map; a collision pass then walks the label boxes top to bottom and pushes each one below any box it overlaps, so that on that estimate no two label boxes intersect and every box lies inside the viewBox, whose width grows with the longest label and height with the ring. Every coordinate is an integer, so that two builds give the same bytes. The tests build cases of one to twelve nodes, short and long titles mixed, and assert both properties; a theme drawing its own map is expected to keep them.

**Shape and glyph, not colour.** A typed entity is a filled circle, a noteless word (a keyword page) a hollow dashed square, and the edge to a noteless word is dashed too (`stroke-dasharray` on the line, `.map-edge-keyword`). Inside the circle, the type is told by its glyph: the profile declares a glyph name per type (`glyph: screen`), and the default theme maps glyph names to eight simple shapes drawn from an inline sprite, `rectangle` (application, screen, table), `hexagon` (api, endpoint, service), `shield` (rule, constraint, principle, standard), `book` (term), `page` (document, meeting, message), `diamond` (object, item, package, decision), `arrow` (process, batch, channel, event) and `triangle` (goal, requirement, milestone, strategy). A glyph name the theme has no shape for is drawn as its initial, so that every typed node still carries a mark. The mapping lives in the theme (`glyphs.ts`) and is keyed by glyph names, which are profile data: the theme never tests a type slug. Colour is applied by the stylesheet alone (`--color-label` for the nodes, `--color-ink` for the labels, `--color-surface` for the glyphs, `--color-soft` for the frame and the label halo, the accent for the centre alone: it is the current position) and carries no information of its own.

**Beyond the cap.** The build passes `total`, how many one-hop neighbours the entity has in the model. The map is drawn as soon as one neighbour is listed; when the total exceeds the number listed (the neighbourhood is truncated to `site.neighbourhood.size`, six by default and twelve at most), a sentence under the map says so, and the list of the neighbours that were kept follows with its note. A page without any neighbour draws no map: the notice that none is recorded links to the mentions panel (`#mentions-title`) instead.

**In the panel.** On the entity page and the keyword page the slot sits inside the `<details class="neighbourhood-fold">` at the foot of the panel. Its summary holds two wordings the stylesheet swaps on the state of the disclosure: closed, a small network mark (`<svg class="neighbourhood-icon">`, decorative, the centre in the accent) before "See the neighbourhood map · N pages", N counting the nodes the map draws; open, a back control drawn as ◂ (the marker of the summary), the title of the map and the name of the page, the heading of the section then kept for assistive technology only. From the desktop width (`68.75rem`) the open fold replaces the other blocks of the panel (`.entity-side:has(> .neighbourhood-fold[open]) > .panel-block` is hidden) and the panel column widens from 21.5 to 26.875 rem to hold the frame of the map (`.entity:has(> .entity-side > .neighbourhood-fold[open])`), so that the map takes the panel and the text stays readable beside it; on a phone the fold stays in the flow of the page under the blocks, on a tablet at the foot of the page where the [layout](#the-default-theme) places it, the map no wider than the panel. Without any script the disclosure simply unfolds under the blocks. The distance control lists the hops the model records, one today: the displayed neighbourhood is computed at one hop and the theme computes nothing in the browser.

## Accessibility

The default theme is built so that a screen reader or keyboard user reaches the same information as everyone else, and the tests hold it to five guarantees. A theme that overrides a slot inherits them only if it keeps what they rest on; this section says what that is. The [accessibility guide](accessibility.md) lists what is verified criterion by criterion, the test file that enforces each one, and what a theme author must keep.

**Contrast.** `contrastRatio(a, b)` and `relativeLuminance(colour)` implement the WCAG definitions; `contrastPairs(theme)` lists, for both schemes of a `theme.yaml`, the pairs the default stylesheet draws: `ink`, `muted`, `label` and `accent` text over `bg`, `surface` and `soft`, and `ink` over `highlight` (a marked passage), each with the minimum of its use, 4.5:1 for body, muted, label and link text and for a mark, 3:1 for headings and the focus ring (`CONTRAST_MINIMUMS`); `paletteColours` derives the three optional colours first. `checkContrast(theme)` returns the pairs under their minimum; the gallery prints them as warnings and the site build will. The default palettes pass every pair with room, and a test pins their measured ratios: in the light palette, body text from 13.8:1 (over the page) to 16.1:1 (over a surface), secondary text from 9.2:1, the lightest labels from 4.52:1, links from 5.15:1; in the dark palette, body text from 12.68:1, secondary text from 6.81:1, labels from 4.92:1, links from 4.94:1; nothing under 4.5:1 in either, and every dark ratio is pinned on its own, none deduced from the light scheme. The light accent, `#A8431C`, is darker than the `#C24E24` of the mark because links are drawn in accent over the page background, where the mark's colour does not reach 4.5:1. A theme author changing a colour runs `concordance gallery` and reads the `contrast:` lines; borders are decorative and not checked, a control being identified by its label, not by its outline.

**Focus and keyboard.** Every interactive element (`a`, `button`, `input`, `select`, `textarea`, `summary`, anything with `tabindex`) shows the same `:focus-visible` ring in the base layer, a 3 px outline in `--color-accent` offset by 2 px; no rule of the shipped stylesheets removes an outline, and a project stylesheet must not either without drawing an equivalent. Every control, link of the chrome and summary is a target of at least 40 px, and no text of the shipped stylesheets goes under 13 px; a test reads both. The skip link is the first element of the body and appears on focus. The tab order is the document order: the default theme sets no positive `tabindex` and no inline handler, and each page reads skip link, header, main, footer. The tree of the space and every block of the panel are native `details` elements with a named `summary`; the pages beyond the inline mentions are reachable through a plain link to their fragment before hydration and through a real `button` after it.

**Textual equivalents.** Every graphical view ships the same information as structured text in the served HTML, where the search index reads it. The neighbourhood renders a `<figure>` holding the map (an SVG with `aria-hidden="true"` and `focusable="false"`, integer coordinates so that two builds give the same bytes) and a `<figcaption>`, then, after the figure inside the same `<section>` and under its heading, the head of the list and the `<ul id="neighbourhood-list">`; the figure points at the list with `aria-describedby`, the list is never hidden, and it is authoritative. Each entry names the entity (a link with its title), its type (the `typeLabel` badge, from the profile in the site language, "Keyword" for a noteless word) and the nature of the link (the `relation` label): the build fills every one of them, and reads the relation from the page, so that a link pointing at the page takes the `inverse_label` of its relation when the profile declares one, "accesses" on the screen and "is accessed by" on the object. The default component writes what the view model gives and no wording of its own, so that a project's labels and the profile's translations reach the page unchanged. The list is plain text in the served page, and the [search index](../spec/mvp.md#l3-01-search-index-generated-at-build) indexes the body of every page, this list included. The axe audit of the tests runs over an entity page whose map holds six nodes of every kind and over one where the pointer replaces the map, and expects no violation of any impact. A theme drawing its own map keeps the list, its id and the two attributes.

**ARIA roles and states.** The controls of the related pages form a named `role="group"` and their page count an `<output>` element, a status region by nature; the tree of the space is a `<nav>` named "Tree of the space" whose current page carries `aria-current="page"`, as does the last step of the breadcrumb; the disclosure buttons of the operations of the contract viewer carry `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, the schema buttons of the viewer `aria-pressed`; every `<details>` of the page starts with a named `<summary>`; the two search forms are `role="search"` landmarks with distinct names; a tab strip, when a theme adds one, uses `role="tablist"`, `role="tab"` with `aria-selected` and `aria-controls`, and `role="tabpanel"` labelled by its tab. The static checker enforces these patterns on every page with four rules next to the nine structural ones: `aria-expanded-on-toggles` (a button or summary with `aria-controls` states `aria-expanded`, `true` or `false`, and controls an existing id), `details-summary` (a `details` starts with a named `summary`), `tab-roles` (tabs inside a tablist, each with a state and a panel labelled by it, no orphan panel), `focusable-has-visible-name` (a focusable element whose visible content is hidden from assistive technologies, an icon typically, exposes a name through `aria-label`, an SVG `<title>`, an image alternative, a `title` or a label).

**Automated audit.** The site tests run axe-core over every page of the gallery, the index included, in a DOM without a layout engine (`happy-dom`), and fail on any violation of impact serious or critical; the default theme currently has none of any impact. The rules that need layout, `color-contrast`, `color-contrast-enhanced` and `link-in-text-block`, are disabled there and covered by the contrast test, which reads the palette the stylesheet declares. A theme author runs the same suite on an overriding component by adding it to the gallery fixtures of a test.

## Budget

`measureBudget(pages, islands, { maxPageBytes })` compares every page with the budget, 150 kB excluding previews, and lists the pages over it. Its summary gives one line per island (`island mentions-panel: 12.0 kB`), the number of pages with the largest one, and one line per page over budget; the build prints it and continuous integration fails on any page over budget.
